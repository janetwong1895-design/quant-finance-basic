## Research-card analytic rubric for a future human review

Use four dimensions scored 0, 1 or 2. This rubric is not presented as an automated grader or a current required instructor service.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reproducible comparison | Rule or baseline absent | Named but allocation or holding timing ambiguous | Exact allocation, start/hold rule and baseline specified |
| Comparable evidence | Cherry-picked or mismatched comparison | Some assumptions stated, others ambiguous | Same scenarios, initial value and declared cost convention; measurable comparison |
| Interpretation and limits | Claims guaranteed real performance | Conditional result with generic limit | Trade-off explained; material assumption and weakening evidence named |
| Feedback response | No revision or unsupported assertion | Edit named without rationale | Specific edit and reason tied to testability or evidence |

Critical errors needing correction regardless of total: presenting synthetic outcomes as real-world proof; hiding an unfavourable scenario; choosing a baseline solely to flatter the result. Any consequential future judgement requires human review and an appeal route; this prototype awards no credential.

## Calibration examples

Strong: “Buy 50/40/10 once and hold; compare with 20/40/40 over both synthetic one-year scenarios, starting at £1,000 and zero costs. Record returns and their percentage-point differences. The steady case gives up 1.8 points; stress loss improves by 6.3 points. Different returns or real fees could reverse the trade-off. I changed ‘safer investment’ to ‘smaller stress loss in the supplied scenario’.” Expected 8/8 despite reporting a losing stress result.

Weak: “My allocation is best because the graph rises. I selected the worst fund as the benchmark. The toy return proves I can make money.” Expected 0/8; revise the comparison, scope and limitations before claiming quality. Do not award points for positive P&L.

These calibrations are author examples; no two-human grading reliability study has been conducted. Learner timing and rubric thresholds still require pilot calibration.
